IFRS 18, the upcoming IFRS Accounting Standard: what you need to know

Effective: Annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027; early application permitted
View Original Source →

Summary

IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements, introducing fundamental changes to income statement structure and disclosure requirements. The standard mandates classification of income and expenses into three defined categories (operating, investing, financing) with two new mandatory subtotals, requires disclosure of management-defined performance measures (MPMs), and enhances aggregation/disaggregation requirements. It does not alter recognition or measurement principles.

Key Changes

  • Replaces IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements
  • Mandatory classification of all income and expenses into three categories: operating, investing, financing
  • Two new required subtotals: 'Operating profit or loss' and 'Profit or loss before financing and income tax'
  • Disclosure requirements for management-defined performance measures (MPMs) including reconciliation to IFRS-defined subtotals
  • Enhanced general aggregation and disaggregation principles for primary financial statements and notes
  • Specific disaggregation requirements for certain expenses (e.g., depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits) when operating expenses presented by function

Transition Approach

Full retrospective application required; comparative information must be restated

Judgment Areas

  • Classification of income and expenses into operating, investing, or financing categories based on the entity's main business activities
  • Determination of whether revenue from ancillary or multiple business activities falls within the operating category
  • Identification of subtotals in public communications that qualify as management-defined performance measures (MPMs)
  • Application of enhanced disaggregation requirements to determine appropriate level of granularity in notes
  • Assessment of whether operating expense presentation is by nature or function to trigger specific disaggregation requirements

Disclosure Impact

Significant expansion of note disclosures: (1) MPM disclosures including description, calculation, reconciliation to nearest IFRS subtotal, and tax/non-controlling interest effects; (2) Enhanced disaggregation of expenses by nature in notes when operating expenses presented by function; (3) Improved aggregation/disaggregation transparency across primary statements and notes; (4) Potential restatement of comparative period disclosures to align with new classification and MPM framework.
